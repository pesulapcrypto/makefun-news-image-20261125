Notes from the bell desk, kept the way a floor clerk keeps them:
short, dated, and with the source written next to the number.

The opening bell was Nvidia's. Monday's close — and the card says
Monday, in the headline's own ticker, because the bell that rang
was Monday's — put the shares up 31.5% and pulled nearly a trillion
dollars of value back onto the board. Second on the board, the PCE
report: due Tuesday, unwritten at press time. This desk does not
print figures that have not landed, so the card carries a question
mark where the number will go. Third, Hormuz. Iran says three
tankers were mined. Says. The card is stamped CLAIMED and the deck
repeats the claim as a claim; a bell that rings rumours as facts
gets its rope cut.

The rest of the board, in the order the clerk pinned it: AWACS to
Poland with a chancellor's promise behind it; Baghdad reaching for
the Kurdish oilfields; a new count putting Russian war dead past
700,000; seven killed in Gaza, named by role; fifty Boeing jets for
Xiamen; Madrid's five goals; twelve coral species back on the reef
books; storm weather standing over both American coasts for the
holiday; the Solomons shopping for a new treaty partner; and
Brussels tightening the sanctions screw one more turn.

Under the board, the clerk's instruments. A ticker of the day's
eight figures that matter, each with its date attached. Region
counts that sum to the stories actually pinned — no region is
claimed without a card on the board. Twenty-four briefs in strict
number order. The day rung bell to bell in six entries. A dotted
map pinned by card number. And the DATE CHECK box, where five
stories were rung against the date and failed: a game launch, last
month's climate talks, a weekend football score, science filed on
Sunday, and a sale that starts on Friday.

How the page was made: `compose_bell.py` lays the day's locked
reporting pool out two ways — a cream page with a ticker band, and
a green-band page — and the two were compared bell to bell. The
cream page won; the green one's ticker ran off the edge of the
board, which no clerk would sign. `entry.png` is the winner,
rendered twice to the same byte. `main.py` does the carrying and
nothing more.

Sources, as written on the board: Forbes via wires, RFE/RL,
Al Jazeera, DW, Starlit Daily and the wire roundups. The drawings
are this desk's own.
